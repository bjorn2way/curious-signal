- [Abdominal fat predicts heart disease risk better than BMI](https://www.acc.org/about-acc/press-releases/2026/08/11/14/59/abdominal-fat-predicts-heart-disease-risk-better-than-bmi): A study published in the Journal of the American College of Cardiology indicates that waist circumference and waist-to-hip ratio provide superior indicators of cardiovascular disease risk compared to body mass index alone. Researchers analyzed data from over 260,000 participants across an average of 20 years to determine if adding these measurements to BMI improved the prediction of nine major cardiovascular outcomes. The analysis revealed that individuals with normal or overweight BMI classifications who also exhibited high waist circumference or waist-to-hip ratio faced a 15% to 50% greater risk for most studied outcomes. Conversely, individuals with obesity and low waist measurements did not show significantly different risk levels for most outcomes compared to those with normal weight and low waist measurements, except for all-cause mortality which was lower in the obese group with low waist size. Lead author Zeina A. Dardari emphasized that relying solely on BMI can lead to the misclassification of cardiovascular risk, particularly among people with clinically normal weight who possess elevated central adiposity. Senior author Michael J. Blaha noted that traditional BMI thresholds often fail to account for the distribution of body fat, specifically visceral fat surrounding internal organs which is strongly linked to chronic diseases. Editor-in-Chief Harlan M. Krumholz stated that the findings demonstrate where fat is distributed matters significantly and urged clinicians to incorporate central adiposity measures into routine risk assessments. The study authors acknowledged limitations including the lack of data on physical activity, diet, and genetic obesity risk, as well as the use of only a single assessment of waist measurements.

The company explained that the watermarking process creates undetectable patterns in low-stakes word choices, such as selecting between synonyms for weather conditions, which remain invisible to readers but identifiable to those with the appropriate key. Anthropic stated that this method does not impact the quality of Claude’s output and that watermarked responses are indistinguishable from unwatermarked ones. The firm plans to use the SynthID-Text approach developed by Google DeepMind and will release a corresponding detection API. This technique differs from other AI detection services that look for stylistic tells in writing, as it relies on encoded patterns rather than linguistic analysis. Anthropic acknowledged that while light editing might not remove the watermark, a complete rewrite would eliminate it. The company noted that watermarks are less likely to appear in code because the model has fewer arbitrary choices when generating functional programming syntax. Watermarks may still appear in code comments where arbitrary word choices exist, but they will have a negligible effect on the actual code. User reaction to the announcement has been mixed, with some subscribers canceling their accounts and others criticizing the move on social media. Anthropic confirmed that other major model developers have signed the same Code of Practice and will implement their own watermarks.
