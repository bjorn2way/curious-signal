## Top Stories

### AMD acquires Taalas to boost inference performance by etching models in silicon

AMD has acquired AI chip company Taalas to challenge Nvidia’s dominance in AI inference hardware. The deal, announced at market close on Thursday, is an actual acquisition not an acquihire of the Toronto-based startup founded in 2023. **Key Technical Details:** * **Architecture:** Taalas uses Model-Specific Integrated Circuits MSICs that etch model weights directly into silicon mask-ROM recall fabric rather than using High Bandwidth Memory HBM . KV caches and fine-tuning adapters are stored in SRAM. * **Performance:** Taalas’ first test chip HC1 , fabbed on TSMC’s 6nm process, processed Meta’s Llama 3.1 8B at 16,960 tokens per second—48x faster than Nvidia GPUs and 8.5x faster than Cerebras accelerators at the time of testing. * **Future Roadmap:** The second-generation HC2 chip, due this summer, targets 20 billion parameters per chip. A trillion-parameter model would require approximately 50 accelerators using pipeline parallelism. **Strategic Implications:** * **Efficiency:** Taalas chips are significantly more space and power-efficient than Nvidia’s LPX systems, which require dozens of GPUs or ~2,000 Groq LPUs for similar workloads. * **Integration:** AMD plans to pair Taalas tech with its Instinct-based Helios racks in a disaggregated architecture: GPUs handle compute-heavy prompt processing, while Taalas accelerators handle token generation. Alternatively, AMD may use a "tick-tock" cadence, allowing customers to validate models on Instinct before transitioning to Taalas. * **Limitations:** The primary drawback is inflexibility; changing models requires a chip re-spin. However, Taalas claims this only requires changing two metal layers, making it cheaper and faster than starting from scratch. * **Target Market:** The technology is best suited for AI model developers, infrastructure providers, and inference providers who are certain of their model choices. AMD aims to leverage its relationships with major customers like OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta. * **Impact on Development:** Lower costs and higher speeds potentially 10x–20x boosts may encourage the use of "test-time scaling," allowing models to reason longer to reduce hallucinations. The deal is subject to regulatory approval and is expected to close in the fourth quarter. ([AMD acquires Taalas to boost inference performance by etching models in silicon](https://www.theregister.com/systems/2026/08/06/amd-acquires-ai-chip-startup-taalas-to-boost-inference-performance-by-etching-models-into-silicon/5284344)).
